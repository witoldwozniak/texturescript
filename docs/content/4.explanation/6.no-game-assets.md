TextureScript's repository contains no Minecraft textures, models or other game files, and never
will. Those files belong to Mojang. Redistributing them, or anything traced from them, is not
something an open-source tool should do or invite.

Yet the tools need vanilla constantly: to plant wheat beside your corn, to give an agent
reference grids, to measure the conventions the lint enforces. The answer is to read the game
from the copy the user already has.

## How each part follows the rule

- **Scenes** read vanilla blocks from your client jar at run time and keep them in memory.
- **The authoring skill** ships only the authored corn example and a manifest of vanilla
  textures to look at. `fetch` extracts those from your own jar, and refuses to write them
  anywhere git would track them.
- **The lint rules** ship as numbers, measurements of vanilla, not as textures.
- **The pre-commit hook** compares staged PNG and JSON files with your jar and refuses copies,
  re-encodes, nearest-neighbour upscales and model or blockstate JSON taken from the game.

## What this asks of you

Do not commit or publish fetched references, or anything traced from them. Draw your own
textures; use vanilla to compare, not to copy.
