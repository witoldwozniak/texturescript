A palette file declares the materials of one crop family. The plant, its stages and its items
all share it.

```text [corn.palette]
material leaf    dark #266325  light #55ab2d  shades 4  keys abcd
material kernel  dark #a8741a  light #f2d158  shades 3  keys eEF
material tassel  dark #7c7a30  light #d9cf78  shades 3  keys tTU
transparent .
```

## Lines

| line | meaning |
|---|---|
| `material NAME dark #RRGGBB light #RRGGBB shades N keys CHARS` | one material: `N` shades derived between the two anchors, inclusive, with one key per shade in `CHARS`, darkest first |
| `transparent KEY` | the key for empty pixels, usually `.` |
| `# ...` | a comment |

## Rules

- `shades` is 3 to 6, the range vanilla materials use.
- The light anchor must be lighter than the dark one, by at least 15 OKLab lightness units.
- Shades are interpolated in OKLCH, so you never type an interior shade.
- Keys and material names must be unique across the whole file. `#` cannot be a key: it starts a
  comment.

## Output

`texturescript palette FILE.palette` prints a palette section, ready to paste at the top of a
grid:

```text
palette
. transparent
# leaf: 4 shades #266325 -> #55ab2d
# leaf: dark L44 C11 h143, light L66 C18 h138; vanilla foliage: dark L31-57, light L45-79, chroma <= 24
a #266325
b #347a2a
c #43922d
d #55ab2d
```

The second comment line gives each anchor's OKLCH lightness and chroma (×100) and hue in degrees, beside the
envelope measured on vanilla ramps of the same class: foliage, cool or warm produce. It warns
when an anchor is outside.

Re-paste the section into every grid after you change an anchor. `stages` checks every declared
key against the exact generated colour.
