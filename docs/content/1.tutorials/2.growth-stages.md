A crop in Minecraft is not one texture but a sequence of them, one per growth stage. In this
tutorial you work with a finished two-block corn plant, generate all eight of its stages from
one file, change how it grows and look at the result next to vanilla wheat.

You should have finished [Your first texture](/tutorials/first-texture) first.

## Get the corn example

The authoring skill that ships with TextureScript contains a complete corn crop. Export it:

```sh
texturescript skill corn-skill
cd corn-skill/examples/crop
ls
```

```text
corn.palette  corn.stages  corn.toml  mature
```

`mature/` holds the fully grown plant as two grids, `lower.grid` and `upper.grid`, because corn
is two blocks tall. Open `mature/lower.grid` to see the stalk on columns 7 and 8, the leaves and
the ear on the right.

## Read the stages file

Open `corn.stages`. It starts by naming its inputs:

```text [corn.stages]
palette corn.palette
grid lower mature/lower.grid
grid upper mature/upper.grid
stages 8
```

Then four `shift` lines:

```text
shift 0 +2
shift 1 +2
shift 2 +1
shift 3 +1
```

These draw the young stages two or one shades lighter within each material, the way young
vanilla wheat is brighter than ripe wheat.

The rest is two **maps**, one per half. A map is a 16×16 grid in which every opaque pixel of the
mature plant carries the digit of the first stage it appears in. Here is the lower half:

```text
map lower
.......55.......
.......55.......
.......44.......
.......44..77...
..44...44.777...
.4444..44.666...
44..33333.666...
.....3333666....
.......3366.....
.......22.......
.......22...33..
.......11..233..
.33....11.123...
.332...00012....
..221..00.......
...210000.......
```

Read it from the bottom up: at stage 0 there is only the base of the stalk. The stalk climbs a
pair of rows per stage, leaves arrive at stages 2 and 3, the ear (the `6` and `7` block on the
right) at stages 6 and 7. The upper half's map starts at stage 5, so corn becomes two blocks tall
at stage 5.

## Generate the stages

```sh
texturescript stages corn.stages out
```

```text
stage0 lower: pass
stage1 lower: pass
stage2 lower: pass
stage3 lower: pass
stage4 lower: pass
stage5 lower: pass
stage5 upper: pass
stage6 lower: pass
stage6 upper: pass
stage7 lower: pass
stage7 upper: pass
strip: out/strip.png
```

Every half of every stage was rendered and linted, and the seam between the halves was checked.
Open `out/strip.png` to see all eight stages side by side. Each `out/stageN/` folder holds that
stage's grids and PNGs.

## Change how the corn grows

Say the ear should show one stage earlier. In the lower map, the ear's pixels are the `6`s and
`7`s on rows 3 to 8. Change every `7` in those rows to `6`:

```text
.......44..66...
..44...44.666...
```

Run `stages` again into the same folder:

```sh
texturescript stages corn.stages out
```

Open `out/strip.png` again: the tip of the ear is now there at stage 6. You changed the growth
of the whole crop by editing digits, without drawing a single stage by hand.

## See it in 3D, next to vanilla

`corn.toml` is a scene that plants every stage in a row, a small field of mature corn and, if
you have Minecraft installed, vanilla wheat and a sunflower for scale.

```sh
texturescript view corn.toml
```

The scene reads `corn.stages` directly, so you do not need to run `stages` before viewing.
Change a digit in a map and save: the view updates. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom and press `l`
to hide the labels.

Without a browser, take screenshots from two angles instead:

```sh
texturescript shoot corn.toml shot.png --cam 30,22,7 --cam 210,25,9
```

Without Minecraft, add `--no-jar`: the vanilla blocks are then reported as missing and left out.

## What you have learned

A crop's stages come from one mature drawing plus a map of when each pixel appears. Shifts make
young stages lighter, `stages` renders and lints them all, and a scene shows them on the real
block model. To go further:

- [Hand-edit one stage](/how-to/hand-edit-a-stage) when a pixel needs to move, not just appear.
- [The stages file](/reference/stages-files) lists every directive, including `recolour`.
- [Why growth stages are maps](/explanation/growth-stages) explains the design.
